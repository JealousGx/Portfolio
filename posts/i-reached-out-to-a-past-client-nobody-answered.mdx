A couple of years ago I built something solid for a client, from scratch. The kind of project I'd have expected to open a door somewhere down the line. It went well, as far as I know. They seemed happy. I moved on to other things, they moved on with their business, and that was that.

This week I reached out again. Nothing complicated, just a message asking if they had any new work, or knew anyone who did.

Nothing came back. Not a no. Not a "we'll keep you in mind." Just silence. Message read, no reply.

## What I Told Myself

I don't think it's personal. People get busy. Priorities shift. The person who worked with me directly might not even be there anymore, might have moved teams, might not even remember the project clearly enough to know who to point toward. I know all of that. I could list five reasonable explanations without much effort.

None of that stopped a small, dumb part of me from expecting something back. We worked together. It went well. It felt like that should count for more than it apparently does, once the project ends and everyone's attention moves somewhere else.

## The Thing Nobody Tells You

I used to think good work was the whole equation. Do the job well, and the rest takes care of itself, referrals show up, past clients think of you when something new comes along, the relationship carries forward on its own momentum.

It turns out finishing a project well doesn't automatically keep a door open. It gets you the door. Whether it stays open depends on things that have nothing to do with how good the work was, timing, memory, whether anyone on the other end is even thinking about hiring right now.

Nobody warns you about this part when you start doing this kind of work. You hear "do good work and the clients will come," and it's not wrong exactly, it's just incomplete. Sometimes you have to knock again. And sometimes you knock, and knock well, and nobody answers anyway.

## What I'm Actually Sitting With

I don't have a clean takeaway here. No five-step plan for turning silence into a callback. I know logically that one unanswered message means very little, it's not rejection, it's just nothing, which is somehow harder to process than an actual no would be. A no gives you something to respond to. Silence just sits there.

What I do know is that I'm not going to stop reaching out because of one quiet message. If anything, this is the reminder that a relationship from a couple of years ago isn't something you can just leave dormant and expect to still be warm when you need it. It needs tending, even the good ones. Especially the good ones, maybe, since those are the ones you assume will just be there.

Not sure what to do with any of this except keep going. Send the next message. Build the next thing. Let the silence be what it is instead of turning it into a story about what I did wrong.

---

*If you've worked with someone before and things went well, tell them. It costs nothing and it might be the difference between a door staying open and one that quietly closes without anyone deciding to close it.*
